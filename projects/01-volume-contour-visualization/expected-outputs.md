# Expected Outputs

## What A Successful Render Should Show

- a slice through the scalar volume
- visible variation in `temperature`
- contour lines or isosurfaces based on the selected scalar threshold
- a scalar bar with the array name
- a clean, framed screenshot suitable for review

## What The Image Can Support

| Observation | What It Means | What It Does Not Mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smooth gradient | The scalar field changes continuously across space | It does not prove a real physical process |
| Contour structure | Specific threshold values are present | It does not identify a cause on its own |
| Bright and dark regions | The chosen color map encodes low and high values | It does not imply importance by itself |

## Practical Limitations

- The dataset is mathematically generated.
- The visual result depends on the selected slice plane.
- Contour thresholds and color-range choices affect the look of the output.
- A polished image is not a substitute for a real analysis context.
